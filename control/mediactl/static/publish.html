Stream your game

Share your Balatro game with the tournament. Nobody sees it live: it reaches the casters later. Pick one of three ways:

Logged in as

Sound

Balatro, Discord and anything else playing on your PC. After you share the Balatro window, click Add computer sound, pick Entire screen and tick Share system audio.

Why the entire screen? Browsers can't take the sound of one program, and a shared window comes without sound. The only way they offer your computer's sound is together with your entire screen. This page uses only the sound of that share: viewers still see nothing but your Balatro window. (Want only Balatro's and Discord's sound? Stream with OBS instead.)
Your own voice. Only asks for your microphone, never your camera.
Not streaming.
Tips
  • Use Chrome or Edge. Firefox can't share computer sound.
  • Keep this tab open while you play. It can be in the background.
  • Switching with your teammate: stop here (or close the tab) first, then they start with their own login. While you're live, theirs is refused.
  • If your connection drops, this page reconnects by itself.

Stream with OBS

You need the OBS address from your login details: it starts with srt:// and already contains your login, so there's nothing to enter here. Don't stream from OBS and the browser at the same time.

Is your OBS already streaming to Twitch? OBS can only stream to one place at a time: choose Via your Twitch stream above instead.

  1. Settings → Stream: Service Custom..., Server: paste your OBS address. Leave Stream Key empty.
  2. Settings → Output: set Output Mode to Advanced. On the Streaming tab: Video Encoder x264 or a hardware H.264 encoder (NVIDIA NVENC H.264, AMD H.264, QuickSync H.264), not HEVC or AV1. Bitrate 6000 Kbps, Keyframe Interval 2 s, and no B-frames: Max B-frames 0 (NVIDIA/AMD/QuickSync), or for x264 type bframes=0 under x264 Options. Browsers can't play video with B-frames, so without this casters can't watch you in a browser.
  3. Settings → Video: Output (Scaled) Resolution 1920x1080 (or 1280x720), FPS 60 (or 30).
  4. Picture: in Sources, click + → Game Capture (mode "Capture specific window", pick Balatro), or Window Capture if Game Capture stays black.
  5. Sound: OBS includes all your computer's sound (Desktop Audio) by default. For only Balatro and Discord: in Sources, click + → Application Audio Capture twice, pick Balatro and then Discord, and mute Desktop Audio in the Audio Mixer. Your microphone is Mic/Aux: mute it if you don't want it.
  6. Click Start Streaming. To hand over to your teammate, click Stop Streaming first. OBS reconnects by itself if your connection drops.
  7. Test it: log in at the top and watch your test feed below.

Stream via your Twitch stream

Keep streaming to Twitch with OBS as usual. While your team is on a tournament feed, the casters see your Twitch stream.

  1. Required: delay your Twitch stream by exactly the tournament delay.
    The tournament delay is now 15 minutes = 900 seconds.
    In OBS: Settings → Advanced → Stream Delay: tick Enable, set Duration to 900 seconds, and tick Preserve cutoff point when reconnecting. OBS only applies it when a stream starts: if you're live already, stop your stream and start it again.
    • Why: without it, your opponents could watch your game live.
    • Why exactly: the casters show your game next to your opponent's. A longer or shorter delay puts your game out of step with theirs.
    • If the tournament delay changes, this number changes too (this page keeps it up to date): change OBS and restart your stream.
  2. Connect your Twitch channel with your login (above):
  3. Strongly recommended: also stream to the tournament directly. Your Twitch stream isn't recorded, so without this your games are missing from the tournament's archive and VODs. It doesn't change what the casters see. Choose In this browser above and go live there as well, at the same time as Twitch (pick the Balatro window itself, not OBS's preview). Your Twitch stream isn't affected.
  4. Test it: watch your test feed below. It shows your Twitch stream, the way the casters will.
Good to know
  • The casters see your whole Twitch stream: overlays, webcam and scene switches included. Keep Balatro on screen during your matches.
  • Ad breaks show as a pause screen on the feed: avoid running ads during your matches.
  • Twitch itself adds a few seconds of lag, so your game can be slightly behind your opponent's.
  • Optional: setting Max B-frames to 0 in OBS's encoder settings (x264: bframes=0 under x264 Options) lets the casters watch your stream in a browser too; in OBS it works either way.
  • Handing over to your teammate: they connect their own Twitch channel here with their login, or click Stop using it and stream another way.

Test your setup (required once per team)

Your test feed is exactly what the casters will get from you: your game, 15 minute(s) after you play it (the tournament delay). Until then it shows the tournament's waiting screen. Only your team can watch it.

  1. Go live (above), the way you chose.
  2. Watch your test feed and check that you see and hear your game.
  3. Done: the banner at the top turns green by itself once the tournament has received your stream.

One tested player per team is enough: the one who will stream your games. If you'll take turns streaming, both test.

It stops by itself after 3 minutes, to keep the server free for other players: watch again if you need more time. Keep it muted while you stream your computer sound, or its sound ends up in your stream. Tip: open this page on your phone and watch it there instead.
